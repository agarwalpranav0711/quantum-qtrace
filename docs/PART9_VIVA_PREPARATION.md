## 1. Domain & Project Overview Questions

### Q1: What is Q-TRACE and what real-world problem does it solve?
**Answer:** Q-TRACE (Quantum-Assisted Digital Image Forensics and Evidence Intelligence Platform) is a local-first software workstation designed for motor-vehicle insurance claims investigations. It addresses the epidemic of digital image manipulation (staged accidents, duplicated scratches, and spliced damage) by providing an evidence-linked investigation environment. It preserves original evidence through SHA-256 WORM storage, runs classical forensic analyses (ELA, Noise, CMFD), conducts quantum micro-patch comparison experiments, and compiles court-admissible forensic dossiers.

### Q2: Why does Q-TRACE refuse to provide an automated "fraud probability score"?
**Answer:** In legal proceedings (under the Daubert and Frye standards for expert evidence), a binary or percentage fraud score generated by a machine learning black-box is legally indefensible. Physical intent cannot be inferred algorithmically; an anomalous compression artifact could be caused by cloud photo synchronization rather than malicious forgery. Q-TRACE operates as an **examiner decision-support platform**, separating objective technical measurements from subjective human hypotheses.

## 2. Classical Forensics & Signal Processing Questions

### Q3: Explain the mathematical principle behind Error Level Analysis (ELA).
**Answer:** JPEG compression operates on $8 \times 8$ blocks using the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) followed by quantization. When an image is saved repeatedly at the same quality factor $Q$, the rate of error between successive resaves decays toward an equilibrium. If a foreign patch from another image is spliced into a high-quality base, its compression history conflicts with the host image. Re-compressing the image at a known quality (e.g. $Q=95$) and computing the absolute difference $\Delta(x, y) = |I_{orig}(x, y) - I_{recomp}(x, y)|$ reveals higher error residuals in regions that were spliced or resaved at different compression factors.

### Q4: How does Q-TRACE mitigate false positives on metallic vehicle panels?
**Answer:** Metallic car coatings contain aluminum flakes that create high-frequency noise spikes, and curved chrome contours cause DCT edge ringing. Q-TRACE mitigates these through:
1. **Luminance edge masking:** Attenuates ELA residuals along sharp Sobel gradient edges.
2. **Median-filtered noise floor normalization:** Computes spatial variance relative to the global interquartile noise floor.
3. **Consensus Fusion:** Requires spatial overlap (IoU $\ge 0.20$) across independent physical domains (compression + sensor noise) before elevating a candidate region to high confidence.

### Q5: How does the Keypoint CMFD algorithm work and why is it resilient to rotation and scaling?
**Answer:** Q-TRACE uses the Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) to extract local scale-space extrema and 128-dimensional gradient orientation descriptors. Because SIFT descriptors are invariant to illumination, rotation, and scaling, cloned regions that have been resized or rotated can still be matched. Matches are filtered using Lowe's ratio test ($d_1 / d_2 < 0.75$) and clustered into parallel spatial displacement vectors ($D > 30\text{ px}$) to eliminate local texture repetitions.

## 3. Quantum Computing & Information Theory Questions

### Q6: What is the Novel Enhanced Quantum Representation (NEQR) and why is it preferred over FRQI?
**Answer:** The Flexible Representation of Quantum Images (FRQI) stores pixel intensities in the probability amplitudes of a single qubit via rotation angles ($\theta$). FRQI requires continuous-angle state preparation and cannot easily undergo reversible bitwise arithmetic. In contrast, NEQR (Zhang et al., 2013) encodes pixel intensities in a dedicated computational basis register of $q$ qubits ($|C_3 C_2 C_1 C_0\rangle$) entangled with spatial coordinate qubits ($|Y_1 Y_0 X_1 X_0\rangle$). This enables standard reversible digital logic (CNOT, Toffoli gates) to operate on pixel intensities.

### Q7: Explain the difference between Q-TRACE's 16-qubit and 12-qubit architectures.
**Answer:** 
- The **16-Qubit Architecture** uses 4 spatial coordinate qubits, 4 original intensity qubits, 4 modified intensity qubits, and 4 dedicated difference qubits. Eight transverse CNOT gates map the bitwise XOR into the dedicated register, preserving the original inputs. Statevector memory is $1.05\text{ MB}$.
- The **12-Qubit Architecture** eliminates the dedicated difference register by performing the XOR operation in-place directly into the modified intensity register. This reduces the physical qubit count by $25\%$ and decreases classical simulation memory by $16\times$ ($65.5\text{ KB}$).

### Q8: What is the Coupon Collector problem and how does it apply to Q-TRACE?
**Answer:** When measuring a quantum superposition of $N$ spatial coordinates, each measurement shot randomly collapses the state into one coordinate. To reconstruct the complete difference image, all $N$ coordinates must be observed at least once. For $N=16$ coordinates ($4 \times 4$ patch), the expected number of shots is $E[T] = 16 \sum_{i=1}^{16} \frac{1}{i} \approx 54.09\text{ shots}$. By tail bound analysis, $S \ge 155$ shots guarantees with $\ge 99.9\%$ certainty that all 16 coordinates are observed.

### Q9: Does Q-TRACE claim a quantum speedup? How do you justify its performance?
**Answer:** **No, Q-TRACE explicitly does not claim a quantum speedup.** Our empirical benchmarks show that classical SIMD difference calculation executes in $76.01\ \mu\text{s}$, while simulating the 16-qubit circuit takes $100.66\text{ ms}$ ($\sim 1,324\times$ slower). Simulating quantum unitaries on a classical CPU scales exponentially ($O(2^n)$). Q-TRACE's quantum comparator serves as an algorithmic correctness prototype and hardware-readiness implementation demonstrating how quantum image arithmetic would execute on real fault-tolerant quantum hardware.

## 4. Software Architecture & Security Questions

### Q10: How does Q-TRACE guarantee evidence immutability (WORM)?
**Answer:** Q-TRACE implements software WORM storage by computing hardware SHA-256 digests upon ingestion, storing files with NTFS read-only attributes (`stat.S_IREAD`), and validating physical file hashes before executing any analysis. Any unauthorized bit modification is immediately detected, preventing evidence tampering.

### Q11: How does Q-TRACE defend against decompression bomb attacks?
**Answer:** Decompression bombs contain sparse headers claiming massive pixel dimensions (e.g. $30,000 \times 30,000$ pixels), designed to crash servers via memory exhaustion. Q-TRACE enforces `Image.MAX_IMAGE_PIXELS = 100,000,000` and validates image dimensions against strict limits ($\le 10,000\text{ px}$) before allocating decompressed image buffers.

### Q12: Why is Q-TRACE designed as a local-first application rather than a cloud SaaS?
**Answer:** Legal and privacy compliance. Vehicular insurance claims contain sensitive Personally Identifiable Information (PII), vehicle identification numbers (VIN), license plates, and precise EXIF GPS coordinates. Cloud-based SaaS platforms introduce data leak vulnerabilities and regulatory hurdles (GDPR, CCPA). Q-TRACE executes entirely on the investigator's local workstation with zero external network transmission.
